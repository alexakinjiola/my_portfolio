My Story

The journey that
shaped who I am

01

I didn't start out wanting to be a designer. I wanted to understand why some products feel effortless and others feel like a fight. That curiosity became a career.

Every chapter below is a turning point — a moment that pushed my thinking forward and shaped how I approach every project today.

2017 – 2021

The Foundation

Growing up in Lagos, I was obsessed with how things worked. I took apart radios, rebuilt old computers from parts markets in Computer Village, and spent late nights reading about the internet. When I got into Computer Science at the University of Lagos, I finally had a name for what I loved: software.

But somewhere between data structures and algorithms, I realised I cared less about the machines and more about the people using them. My final year dissertation — designing accessible e-government interfaces for low-literacy Nigerians — was the moment everything clicked.

2020 – 2021

Learning to Build & Design

While finishing my degree, I joined Semicolon Africa's competitive bootcamp — selected from 2,400 applicants — and simultaneously completed Google's UX Design Professional Certificate. For the first time, I could speak both languages: design and code. I stopped thinking of them as separate disciplines and started seeing them as one.

That hybrid perspective became my biggest asset. I could sketch a wireframe in the morning and ship it as a working component by evening.

2021 – 2022

Going Global with Andela

Being selected for Andela meant working on real enterprise products with senior engineers in New York and Warsaw. It forced me to write cleaner code, communicate across time zones, and understand how large-scale product decisions get made. I grew more in 10 months there than in 3 years before it.

I also began noticing how rarely engineering teams had anyone who could bridge design intent and implementation — and decided that's exactly where I wanted to live.

2022 – Now

Building for Africa & Beyond

Since going independent in 2023, I've worked with startups across Lagos, Accra, London, and Berlin — helping them design and ship products that actually work for their users. Whether it's a fintech app for first-generation bankers in Kano or an LMS for rural schools without reliable internet, I care deeply about building for the context, not just the prototype.

I'm not done. I'm just getting started.

What I Stand For

The principles that
guide my work

01
🎯
Clarity over cleverness

A great interface is invisible. I resist the urge to show off in favour of making things that just work — for everyone, every time.

02
🧪
Test early, ship often

I'd rather put a rough prototype in front of a real user on day three than present a polished design on day thirty that solves the wrong problem.

03
🌍
Context is everything

A product built for Silicon Valley doesn't automatically work in Lagos. I design for the actual environment — bandwidth, literacy, device, culture.

04
🤝
Collaboration > heroics

The best outcomes come from genuine partnership with clients, engineers, and end users — not from a designer working in isolation and presenting a "reveal."

05
♿
Accessibility is non-negotiable

Designing only for people who look like you is a failure of imagination. I build inclusive products from the first wireframe, not as a final audit.

06
📈
Outcomes over outputs

I care about whether the thing I shipped actually moved the needle — not just that it shipped on time and looked good in the case study.

How I Work

My design & build process

1
Discover

User interviews, analytics audits, competitive analysis, and stakeholder workshops to understand the real problem — not the assumed one.

2
Define

Synthesise findings into a clear brief, user journey maps, and measurable success metrics before any design work begins.

3
Design & Build

Rapid wireframes → interactive prototypes → high-fidelity screens → production code. Each stage is validated with real users.

4
Iterate

Post-launch monitoring, A/B tests, and follow-up research sessions. Shipping is the beginning of the process, not the end.
