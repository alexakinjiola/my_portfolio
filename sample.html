ShopFlow — E-Commerce Redesign

The Challenge

ShopFlow, a Lagos-based e-commerce platform serving 50,000+ monthly users, was haemorrhaging revenue. Conversion rates sat at 1.2% — well below the industry average of 3.5% — and cart abandonment had climbed to 78%. Users found the checkout confusing, product pages uninspiring, and the mobile experience unusable.

My Process

I began with a 2-week discovery phase: moderated usability sessions with 12 users, analysis of session recordings (Hotjar), and a competitive audit of 6 comparable platforms. The findings revealed 3 critical failure points — unclear trust signals, too many steps in checkout, and poor product photography standards.

From there, I designed a revised information architecture, wireframes, and a full high-fidelity design system in Figma. I then led the front-end rebuild in React, including a custom checkout flow, product page template, and real-time inventory component.

Key Outcomes

Within 60 days of launch, conversion rate rose from 1.2% to 1.8% (a 50% relative improvement), and cart abandonment fell from 78% to 37.5%. The redesigned checkout reduced average completion time from 4.5 minutes to 1.8 minutes.

LearnPath LMS

The Challenge

Rural Nigerian secondary schools had tablets and laptops but unreliable internet. Existing LMS platforms like Google Classroom required constant connectivity, making them unusable outside major cities. LearnPath needed to work offline — fully — and sync when connectivity returned.

My Process

Built as a Progressive Web App using React and a service worker strategy for offline caching. Content (videos, PDFs, quizzes) was downloaded on-demand during connectivity windows. A background sync queue handled submissions and progress tracking offline.

Firebase was used for real-time sync when online, with IndexedDB as the offline store. The PWA install experience was optimised to feel native on low-end Android devices.

GrowthCo — Onboarding Redesign

The Challenge

GrowthCo's analytics SaaS had strong retention among activated users but was losing 68% of new sign-ups within 72 hours. Time-to-value averaged 14 days. The CEO suspected the problem was the product; the data suggested it was the onboarding.

My Process

I conducted a full UX audit — session recordings, drop-off funnel analysis in Amplitude, and 8 user interviews with churned customers. The root cause was clear: the onboarding asked users to configure too much before delivering any value. Users gave up before they saw why the product mattered.

I redesigned the activation sequence around a "first aha moment" — getting each user to see one meaningful insight within 5 minutes of sign-up. The redesign included a new onboarding checklist, progressive disclosure of configuration, and an in-app coach layer.

3 days

+41%

Key Outcomes

Onboarding drop-off fell from 68% to 15%. Time-to-value went from 14 days to 3. Week-one retention improved by 41%. The changes contributed to a 22% increase in paid conversion within the next quarter.

Zara Lagos — Brand Identity

The Brief

Zara Lagos, an Afro-fusion restaurant expanding from 1 to 4 locations across Lagos, needed a brand identity that felt premium but rooted in Nigerian culture — not the generic Afrobeats clichés found on every highstreet restaurant sign.

My Process

The creative strategy was built around the concept of "Refined Heritage" — using traditional Yoruba textile patterns as the foundation for a modern geometric logo system. The colour palette was drawn from Aso-Oke weaving: deep indigo, burnt terracotta, and warm gold.

Deliverables included the primary logo and variants, a full typographic system (pairing Canela with Söhne), a 40-page brand guidelines document, packaging mockups, signage specs, and a digital social media kit.

How much do you charge for a typical project? ▾
Every project is scoped individually. For UI/UX design engagements I typically start around $1,500, while full-stack builds range from $3,000 to $20,000+ depending on complexity and duration. I offer fixed-price scopes for well-defined projects and a day-rate ($400/day) for ongoing consulting. The best way to get an accurate figure is to share your brief via the form above.
What's your typical project timeline? ▾
A focused UX audit or design sprint typically takes 2–4 weeks. A full product design engagement (research → design system → high-fidelity screens) usually takes 6–12 weeks. Full-stack builds range from 6 weeks to 4 months. I also take on longer-term retainer arrangements. Timelines depend heavily on feedback speed and scope changes, which we'll agree upfront.
Do you work with clients outside Nigeria? ▾
Absolutely — in fact, roughly 40% of my clients are based outside Nigeria (UK, US, Germany, Canada). I work fully remotely and am comfortable collaborating across time zones. I'm based in WAT (UTC+1), which overlaps comfortably with European hours and has a 4–5 hour overlap with East Coast US mornings.
How do you handle revisions? ▾
All projects include clearly defined revision rounds in the proposal — typically 2–3 rounds per deliverable stage. I use structured feedback sessions to keep revisions focused and efficient. Additional rounds beyond the agreed scope are billed at an hourly rate, which we agree upfront. My aim is always to get it right, not just to hit a revision limit.
What does the design-to-handoff process look like? ▾
I deliver Figma files with organised component libraries, annotated specs, and prototype links. For development handoff I use Figma's Dev Mode and provide comprehensive documentation including interaction notes, responsive breakpoints, and design tokens. If I'm also doing the development, handoff is seamless — no translation layer needed.
Can you work with an existing team or codebase? ▾
Yes, and I enjoy it. I'm comfortable joining a team mid-stream — reviewing existing code, adapting to established conventions, and integrating my work cleanly. For design, I can audit and extend an existing design system rather than starting from scratch. I always start with a codebase/design review before proposing changes.

My Story

The journey that
shaped who I am

01

I didn't start out wanting to be a designer. I wanted to understand why some products feel effortless and others feel like a fight. That curiosity became a career.

Every chapter below is a turning point — a moment that pushed my thinking forward and shaped how I approach every project today.

2017 – 2021

The Foundation

Growing up in Lagos, I was obsessed with how things worked. I took apart radios, rebuilt old computers from parts markets in Computer Village, and spent late nights reading about the internet. When I got into Computer Science at the University of Lagos, I finally had a name for what I loved: software.

But somewhere between data structures and algorithms, I realised I cared less about the machines and more about the people using them. My final year dissertation — designing accessible e-government interfaces for low-literacy Nigerians — was the moment everything clicked.

2020 – 2021

Learning to Build & Design

While finishing my degree, I joined Semicolon Africa's competitive bootcamp — selected from 2,400 applicants — and simultaneously completed Google's UX Design Professional Certificate. For the first time, I could speak both languages: design and code. I stopped thinking of them as separate disciplines and started seeing them as one.

That hybrid perspective became my biggest asset. I could sketch a wireframe in the morning and ship it as a working component by evening.

2021 – 2022

Going Global with Andela

Being selected for Andela meant working on real enterprise products with senior engineers in New York and Warsaw. It forced me to write cleaner code, communicate across time zones, and understand how large-scale product decisions get made. I grew more in 10 months there than in 3 years before it.

I also began noticing how rarely engineering teams had anyone who could bridge design intent and implementation — and decided that's exactly where I wanted to live.

2022 – Now

Building for Africa & Beyond

Since going independent in 2023, I've worked with startups across Lagos, Accra, London, and Berlin — helping them design and ship products that actually work for their users. Whether it's a fintech app for first-generation bankers in Kano or an LMS for rural schools without reliable internet, I care deeply about building for the context, not just the prototype.

I'm not done. I'm just getting started.
